# Direct field editing

Dictation and Magic deliver through the field queues below. The retired composition document, draft Insert workflow and whole-field refine path are no longer producers; iOS uses lightweight dictation status alongside the staged dictation and instruction links.

## Shared owners

`DictationRouteSession` owns the engine, speech-mode router and Magic cut for both hosts. Capture and presence remain platform adapters. `DictationPipeline` produces versioned whole-text snapshots; `LiveSegment.joined` assembles punctuation once at the producer boundary. Both field adapters consume `snapshot.text`. Field cleanup reads the document through the same interface as Magic; the keyboard no longer reports split points for app-side transcript cutting.

`SpeechDelivery` owns recognition revision versus new-speech classification. The iOS bounded word-alignment policy was selected after replaying both previous classifiers over two recorded November 11 journal sequences (30 snapshots): their delivered text agreed at every snapshot. Mac's `FieldSpeechScheduling.Target` decides when to release that shared result. Both platforms permit revised-tail aliases: last words heard again as others stay as typed while new speech goes on after them, and Mac releases the revision in an eligible natural pause or at stop. New speech never waits for a held revision: on Mac, a revision whose delivered end cannot be found goes out with the next snapshot that runs past the delivered text (a recorded take held 22 s of continuous speech behind one revised partial word). The journal's `live` lines carry `held=` while a revision is held. The recorded replay and the differing synthetic contract are documented in [shared speech evidence](plan/shared-speech-replay.md). `SpanPlanner` owns span formation and pace; `KeystrokePlanner` supplies Mac movement/settle costs and translates spans into keys. Costs never widen the minimal grapheme diff.

`FieldInsertion` owns capitalization and both insertion boundaries. A separating space before existing following text belongs to the dictated region, survives revisions and is removed with its undo. Existing whitespace and punctuation remain in the host.

Each field queue owns its undo ledger. iOS `EditHistory` includes scoped Magic edits alongside typing; both the Magic inverse and keyboard Undo/Redo operate on that history. Mac keeps its existing queue ledger and inverse recognition.

`DictationTakeCleaner` owns whole-take timeout execution and validation. Field cleanup validates the text read from the document. Transcript-only fallback consumers retain their sentence-context or buffered streaming/retry policies. `CorrectionCapture` owns pending sources, arming, own-write suppression and input-origin attribution. iOS discards unmatched sources because its proxy window is bounded; Mac retains them for a later readable snapshot and bounds polling to the existing 600-second expiry.

Platform differences remain deliberate: iOS edits a shadow document in chunks checked against the proxy (below); Mac uses Accessibility or guarded synthetic keys. Capture lifecycle and Magic field-settling callbacks remain adapter responsibilities. iPhone dictation is staged: cleaned units are typed once each, nothing typed is edited (below).

## Field cleanup and natural pauses

`FieldCleanupSession` owns automatic field reads, cleanup, conditional application and confirmation. Both live and buffered field delivery use it. Buffered takes carry their pending raw words alongside a current field read; `FieldInsertion` places them at the read cursor. Live speech keeps flowing through its existing queue. Cleanup never sends a rewritten transcript over the field: it applies a `ScopedEdit` against what it read. Password, selection, unavailable or unconfirmed reads do not initiate a blind edit.

`FieldCleanupChunks` owns 60-word passages with a 12-word overlap. Later passages read the previous result's overlap and replace it, so overlap is not appended twice. Its coverage frontier advances only from field-confirmed results and follows user changes through the shared `FieldMerge` boundary mapper. Later pauses send only the overlap and newly uncovered suffix; speech arriving during model work remains uncovered. These are measured workload candidates, not validated semantic optima; [recording measurements](plan/field-cleanup-measurements.md) give the evidence and limits. A stop deliberately bypasses coverage and requests a complete pass over the readable field, including long takes. A model result waits while speech resumes. Before application, a new read confirms the field; `FieldMerge` keeps every change made since the read (the user's, speech typed during the model call) and accepts cleanup changes in untouched words. A bounded read (the iPhone's shadow) is merged when the text before the cleaned passage still opens the field as read, so both reads start at the same place; otherwise it stays as typed. Buffered words still to be inserted need the read exactly. A guard rejection concerns part of the text: the text is cleaned again in halves split at a sentence end, down to single sentences, and only a sentence still refused stays as typed (`FieldCleanupChunks.cleanSplitting`, `field.cleanup.split`). On iPhone a later sentence pass that would open with a sentence refused this take starts after it, with it as context (`FieldCleanupSession.refusedSentences`): sent again it was refused again and split each pass into four to six calls, 4–6 s instead of under one (device run 4, the German "um" before a time). The whole-take pass still covers it. Every pass logs one `field.cleanup` line (`sentence`, `final`, `status`: `cleaned`, `keptAsTyped`, `noChange`, `modelFailed` with its `reason`, `fieldChanged`, `short`, `unchanged`, `readRefused`, …; `ms`, `modelMs`, `gapMs`; counts only).

On Mac, `DictationActivity` consumes capture PCM before network backlog. It permits corrections only when a semantic endpoint arrives after 200 ms of 20 ms frames at or below -55 dBFS, and revokes permission on the next louder frame. This candidate was calibrated on one speaker's recordings, not independently validated as a general voice detector. Silence or token gaps alone never release edits. `FieldSpeechScheduling.Target` and the field queue retain their existing recognition classifier, cursor confirmation and edit units; only the speaking gate now follows activity independently of take lifetime.

On iPhone, live dictation no longer runs a field pass: it is staged (#312, below), so cleanup never edits text already in the field. `attachCleanupField(perSentence:)` and the shadow ask (`LiveDictationSnapshot.shadowAsk`, `FieldShadow`) belong to the Mac now. The iPhone's retired in-place sentence pass is kept as history at the end of this file.

Transcript cleanup remains for integrations without an attached readable field, for the Mac panel's buffered fallback, where no field document exists, and for iPhone live dictation (cleaned per unit, staged). The readable Mac hosts attach the shared field owner. Existing transcript-path contracts continue to cover those fallback consumers.

## iOS: one queue owns the field

Every write the keyboard makes goes through one queue per field (`FieldQueue`): the user's keys, deletes, space-bar cursor pans, word-row and undo edits, live dictation and Soniox revisions, typed lines, the host's text, cleanup and Magic. Nothing else inserts, deletes or moves the cursor. The one exception is a cursor walk that reads the field (an instruction's read, or exploring across a seam); it runs only while nothing of the queue's is on its way and the caret is back at the user's cursor.

### The edit model (#299)

1. **Shadow document.** `ShadowDocument` holds the field as known: text, caret, the user's cursor, selection and the tracked regions. The queue simulates every op it sends on it; the observer (`FieldQueue.observe`) feeds it every host callback, idle or not, and classifies the report: it matches, extends (more text than known), stitches (known text, at one other place only, with at least 16 agreeing characters) or is a seam (text the shadow cannot place). A new document identity is not proof of a new field: Notes and Safari issue one when a field becomes empty, WhatsApp once with no visible cause. When the report under the new identity shows the shadow, the queue, the take and the history stay (`identity-kept` in the journal); a seam starts over as for a new field.
2. **Chunks, not characters.** Speech, a Soniox revision, a cleanup and a user key are each one chunk, executed whole: a take's words go in as one insert, a revision near the caret as one delete run and one insert. Every chunk's insert enters as a fast character burst (`ChunkEntrance`, #300, shared with the Mac): one piece per 60 Hz frame (about 17 ms) on every platform, the fastest pace that still visibly types where hosts redraw at 60 Hz (pieces within one frame show as a block; hosts apply a whole burst in order, so accuracy sets no lower bound); one grapheme per piece until the burst would pass 250 ms, then graphemes group, still one piece per frame; a piece never splits a grapheme cluster or CRLF. The burst is part of its chunk: it joins the chunk's flight, there is one check after the last piece (the deadline counts from that piece), the model takes each piece as it goes out, a user's key waits only for the burst to end, and speech appended behind a chunk enters the same way. Hops and the delete run still go out at once. Reduce Motion, a finished take's rest and held speech (`hasUnverifiedInput`) write the insert whole. The piece timing lives in `FieldQueue` (`Burst`, due times from the steps' elapsed time, so a 5 ms ticker catches up instead of stretching the burst).
3. **One place per chunk.** An edit's change is planned when it starts (`SpanPlanner`: the minimal grapheme diff, changes up to three unchanged graphemes apart or in one word form one group). Each chunk is the last group still to do: hops to its place, its deletes as one delete run (n single `deleteBackward` calls) and its insert, then the hop back to the user's caret, so speech and keys can go between chunks. A single change ending at most three characters before the caret is rewritten from the caret (no hop; the few characters between are typed again). A replacement that would empty a line bounded by a real line break keeps the line's last old grapheme until the new text stands before it (a rich editor fills an emptied line or drops its list item).
4. **Hops only within sight.** A hop never goes past what the last report showed on that side (300 characters in WhatsApp and Notes, cut at line breaks; 100 in Safari); a target further away takes several hops. Where a window stops at a line break, one character past it is allowed when the shadow knows a line break there. After each hop the next report is looked at: the caret at the target, the chunk goes on; the caret exactly where the same move lands counted in the other unit (UTF-16 code units in WhatsApp and Notes, graphemes in Safari), the shadow takes it, learns the field's unit and the next hop corrects by what is left. A report that fits where the hop came from, or any place the chunk had the caret at, may be late and proves nothing. Hops that bring the target no closer twice in a row fail the chunk. Right after a move the proxy's own view shows nothing on the side the caret moved toward (WhatsApp, Notes, Safari; the #299 recheck journals): that is no seam, the next hop waits for the report that shows the way, up to the deadline.
5. **No fixed waits.** A chunk's ops go out back to back on the proxy's immediate view (no host needs a wait between a hop and the edit, measured; only an insert's burst pieces are spaced, rule 2). One check follows: a chunk that ends with its hop back is checked by the first host callback whose report shows its end state (the keyboard's own inserts and deletes make none; WhatsApp and Notes answer a whole burst with one callback, Safari with one per stage, the first still at the old place; the host applies a burst in order). A chunk that ends at the caret (a key, speech) is checked by the proxy's view. A report that does not show the chunk is not a seam until the deadline. The deadline is learned per field (`HostProfile` on the shared `HostLatency`, no host table): the latency from a chunk's last op to the callback that showed it, as a running estimate (smoothed mean plus four mean deviations), within 100 ms to 1 s; 400 ms until three samples exist (`checkDeadlineMs`; Safari's slowest burst showed at 348 ms). A chunk gets 4 ms more per grapheme it deletes (`HostLatency.deadlineMs(deletes:)`, an estimate from two samples): a host applies a delete run one delete at a time, and in device run 4 a WhatsApp chunk deleting 23 showed just past its learned 104 ms, so its check failed and a read walk took 1.8 s. A check that runs out backs it off; later samples bring it down. The profile also learns the unit of a move, the widest window on each side and whether callbacks come one per burst or one per stage, and starts over with a new field (not with a sent or cleared draft). The reset entry of the edit journal records what was learned.
6. **A failed check re-reads.** At the deadline the report becomes the model (`resync`: the user's cursor moved, a jump went nowhere, or the report is reseeded). What the chunk was for is planned again against that read; text that cannot be found again drops, and for cleanup the app runs one fresh pass on the text as read (`FieldCleanupSession`, `unplaceable`). Nothing is restored blindly: a lost op is never replayed from the model.
7. **User input wins.** A key waits only for the chunk that is running; behind a chunk whose check is still open it goes at once and joins that check. A word the recognizer's head showed only in part is finished in the key's insert.
8. **Appends never wait (round 4).** Speech at the caret goes right behind a chunk with hops once the chunk's own ops are out, before its check (a piece's next words, or a new piece of a take that typed already). Hosts apply ops in order (measured), so the chunk's check covers them: a callback that shows the chunk's end, with or without the words behind it, is the chunk's; the proxy's view then shows the words. The deadline counts from the chunk's last op until that callback, then from the last op behind it. The next chunk with hops still waits for the check: relative hops compound errors. A failed check whose report shows the chunk's end without the words takes them out of the model (as the keyboard's own change, so a read's changes still lead to it) and types them again: nothing is lost. Any other failure takes the ordinary re-read.

### Kept, with reasons

- **The opaque-selection hold** (`hasUnverifiedInput`, set by `observe` for a `selectionDidChange` that shows no selection, or a `textDidChange` whose windows stayed as they were). Safari's Select All shows no selection, so an automatic delete there would remove the whole draft. Only plain inserts at the caret go (a take's words, the user's keys) until a report changes.
- **Exploring across a seam** (`FieldRecovery` request, `FieldReader` walk; only with `canReadAround`, never while speech is prioritized or a key waits: the key goes and the edit drops). The observer cannot see past the window; an edit whose text lies behind a seam is looked for by a bounded walk, else it drops.
- **The read walk's settle** (`FieldReader.walkSettleMs`, 45 ms). A walk verifies every window by two identical reads of the host after its own move; it is no edit path.
- **The quiet after a tap** (`FieldQueue.quietMs`, 250 ms). After a tap the keyboard's own work resumes once the field held still: ops already on their way can still land at the tap.
- **Tap repairs** (`explainTap`, `tapRaceLanded`, `strayInsertion`). A tap inside a chunk's landing window moves what is still on its way there. When the report has one explanation (the ops before the tap where they were sent, the rest from the tap), the shadow takes it, the keyboard's own stray insert is taken out again with ownership evidence (dictation's stays and the take is reported), and what it deleted at the tap is typed back.
- **The stop's block swap** (`settleFinal`). A fallback when a verified chunk of the final version failed: the untouched take is swapped whole only when the report shows its end before the caret.
- **Speech waits for the check in fields that answer stage by stage** (`HostProfile.callbacks`, learned: Safari). Safari keeps the proxy on its report after a move while an edit sent right behind it lands (the fake's measured model), so no check could see the words there. Until a field's pattern is learned (cold start) speech waits too.
- **The near-caret revision while speaking** (`nearChars`, 60), for hosts without the route's activity admission (`prioritizeSpeech` off); production hosts release revisions in pauses.

Removed by #299: the settle constants between ops (move 45 ms, edit 110 ms, move-after-edit 90 ms, the 115 ms transit), per-grapheme pacing on iOS with a wait and check per piece (`SpanPlanner`'s pace is the Mac's; the entrance of #300 is one chunk with one check), `deleteConfirmationMs`, the zero-offset stale nudge, the lag and revert heuristics (late reports kept as history, the revert onto an older state and its parked unverified edits), the park wait for edits out of sight (3 s), the 150 ms stable report before a new region, and the keys-wait-for-take hold (#268): speech now goes in whole, so a key lands after the words already heard without a hold.

Removed in round 4: the fixed 400 ms check deadline (learned per field now; 400 ms is the cold start), the iPhone cleanup's field read before the model and its re-read before the edit for live runs (the keyboard's shadow and its marked read replace both; the keyboard no longer lands its own work and pauses the queue for a cleanup read), and the app-side merge of those passes (the keyboard merges through the read).

The draft boundary observer (`observeDraftBoundary`) waits for sustained empty-field evidence across the host confirmation window. During that interval it holds automatic writes and keeps incoming speech pending. A nonempty report resumes the draft only if the native field identity and a unique text match agree. A confirmed clear ends the draft and cancels its automatic writes. For a blank line with a verified caret and known line breaks, a bounded cursor read can check the nearest usable line above, or below if the shadow has no usable line above. The read needs a unique match at the neighboring line and fresh evidence at the original caret before automation can continue. With no confirmed caret, the keyboard preserves recoverable text and lets the user continue editing.

Known limit: with a host that applies a burst late and shows stale reports after a tap (the stress fake's harshest mode), a tap inside the landing window of two chunks can leave one cleaned capital at the tap and its old letter gone at the place. The user's own text is never lost there, and the take stays in History.

### Staged dictation (iPhone, #312)

Live dictation on iPhone delivers whole cleaned units into the field and never edits text that is already there. The raw words of a take never reach the field.

- **Producer.** `DictationPipeline` (`stagedLive`) runs `LiveDictationRun` as units, not as a target text: consecutive segments group until a sentence end with at least 6 words, a line break or 40 words. Each unit is cleaned (`submitLive`/`landLive`) and published the moment its cleanup lands, `LiveSegment.ready`, in a snapshot marked `staged`. A unit whose cleanup fails or exceeds `liveTimeout` stands as its raw words (cleanup only tidies); the History entry records the fallback. There is no whole-take pass and no field pass. The Mac and the buffered send modes are unchanged.
- **Pause and stop close the open unit.** An admitted pause (`DictationActivity`: an endpoint, then 200 ms at -55 dBFS or below; `DictationPipeline.boundary`) and the stop close the open unit whatever its length and clean it at once, so a pause delivers what was said before it. Words cut short by a forced split (25 words or 8 s) stay in the head until their sentence ends.
- **Preview.** `StagedDictation` (keyboard side) holds the snapshot: the preview is the units not in the field yet followed by the speaking head, cleaned text replacing raw in place. `KeyboardController.dictationPreview` is the one derivation of what the word row says while dictating (the buffered modes' take-plus-tail text is the same property); `WordRowView` shows it left aligned in two lines, anchored on its newest words (the text keeps its wrapping and sits at the row's bottom edge, so growth pushes the oldest line out at the top). It carries on after the stop until the last unit is typed.
- **Delivery.** The keyboard types the first undelivered unit whose text is final, one at a time and in order, as one `FieldQueue.Edit(atCursor:)` (`requiresVerifiedInsertion`): one entrance burst (`ChunkEntrance`), one check and one `EditHistory` undo step per unit. The user's keys keep priority. The unit leaves the preview when the queue confirms it. `LiveDictationAck.deliveredThrough` reports the last unit in the field.
- **Separator.** `FieldInsertion` (also under `LiveSegment.joined`) places the space and capital, and the queue applies it when the unit starts (`Edit.unit`), from the text its model shows before the caret then, so keys typed before the unit goes are part of what it meets, and a proxy that is blind after a move does not matter. When the model knows nothing before the caret, the take's context decides: the proxy while it ends with what the take typed, else the take's own record (#278). With neither source the separator is left off. A space goes between the unit and a word that follows the caret. The kept-text Insert uses the same path.
- **Keys during delivery.** A key waits only for the running chunk, and a staged unit is one chunk: the keys land at the caret before or after the whole unit, never inside it, and the caret stays where the keys left it (a confirmed unit's remaining caret-only ops are finished before a key goes, never replanned behind it). A unit whose check failed but whose words are in the field (`StagedDictation.landed`: a mutation was issued and the scope or the window shows them) counts as delivered; it is neither typed nor kept again. Anything less certain goes to the kept-text row, never retyped.
- **Recovery.** If an insert is dropped (the field changed, a selection or unverified input, no verified check), the draft is cleared or the keyboard goes away, delivery stops. The unit in flight and the units after it (cleaned where available, otherwise raw) go to the kept-text row with the draft they came from; units that become ready later join it. Nothing is inserted blind. The kept item is the person's choice to Insert. History holds the whole take. A take that typed nothing yet follows the user into another field; one that typed something stays with its field.
- **A newer take** waits until the units of the take before it are typed when that take is complete. One whose end was never seen is kept.
- **Kept Insert** goes in at the caret when the proxy shows no selection, also right after a tap that left the caret where it was (the host's callback then leaves the queue's input unverified): it is typed as a key or speech is. A selection the proxy shows, or an unknown draft boundary, refuses ("Can't insert here — move the cursor", 2 s, the sentence returns). The kept row shows the start of the sentence after a small waveform cue.
- **Preview height** (estimate, not yet measured on the phone): the row keeps its height (27 pt portrait, 44 pt landscape). The preview uses the largest of 13, 12, 11 and 10 pt whose two lines (1.2 x size), a line spacing of 0.1 x size and 1 pt above and below fit: 10 pt in portrait (24 + 1 + 2 x 1 pt of 27), 13 pt in landscape (`WordRowHeight.previewFontSize`, `previewPadding`).

### Dictation and instructions on the queue

The lanes and regions below describe the queue's live-run path, which the buffered send modes still use; iPhone live dictation no longer types raw words or revises them (see staged dictation above).

- **Lanes.** User input goes first, behind at most the running chunk. Edits come next, then live dictation, with snapshots coalesced to the newest per run. While a take is spoken (`prioritizeSpeech`), edits wait for the gaps between words: the route's pause, or a cleanup pass's ask for the shadow (a sentence end, a long pause, the stop), so revised words land at the latest when the next pass asks.
- **Dictation regions.** A live run types at its region's end. Each take is a run of its own in the app: the mic key (or Action Button) begins it, and so does the bridge's first `dictation-debug-line` or `-tail` after a `dictation-debug-stop`; the mic key or a `dictation-debug-replay` ends a bridge take still open first, as its stop would. A take that begins while the previous run still ends waits until that run is complete; stops end runs in take order. Every take starts its first region at the user's caret; text typed by hand between takes stays in front of it. A key or a cursor move closes the region; later raw words start a new region at the user's cursor. A take that typed nothing yet follows the user into another field; one that typed something stops there, drops once, and its later versions are ignored. A revision or cleanup belongs to the run's own text: its changes map onto the regions where they are, and text it adds at the end of the take goes at the end of the run's last region. Regions split by the user's typing or moves are separate pieces: where a snapshot changes the joint between two pieces, the joint belongs to neither. The keyboard reports where the pieces start (`LiveDictationAck.pieces`), and the app cleans each piece on its own. A finished take keeps its regions until its cleanup came (for the last four takes). In a region the user edited, each change touching a word the user changed is dropped; every other change still lands (`FieldMerge`), leaving the user's characters alone.
- **Kept text (iPhone).** The recovery row preserves undelivered speech (`Drop.kept`) or a committed cleanup result when delivery cannot finish. The App Group store keeps the item across draft changes and keyboard reopening. Copy leaves the item in place. Insert at cursor uses the verified edit queue and clears the item only after confirmation. After an ambiguous write, Copy and Dismiss remain available while a repeat insert is withheld to avoid duplicating text. A retained cleanup rewrite can be copied or dismissed. Undelivered speech takes priority when both need recovery.
- **Instructions (Magic).** A read for an instruction waits (bounded) until the queue's own writes have landed, and marks the model (`markRead`). The edit is placed through every change since that read: a user change inside its scope cancels it; the keyboard's own changes there never do, and are merged into the result where Magic's change does not touch them. Cleanup may change hand-typed text only when that text was in the cleanup's input.

Internal builds journal every queue event with full text (`EditJournal`; see `scripts/device/README.md`). A pulled journal replays against the queue in a test. Every keyboard controller writes it, and an old one can still write while a new one does (device run 4: overwritten lines, and lines in the previous file after a rotation): `RotatingLineFile` (shared with the Mac's journal) appends each batch of lines whole at the file's end under a lock (`edit-journal.jsonl.lock`) and follows a rotation another writer made.

## macOS

One queue per field (`MacFieldQueue`) makes every change: live words and their revisions, cleanup, typed lines, Magic and its undo, and the panel's final typed insert. Nothing else writes to the field.

### The Mac edit model (#301, #300)

- **Whole-field shadow (kept difference).** The Mac keeps the field's whole text and selection (`MacFieldShadow`), not the iPhone's windowed `ShadowDocument`: its AX read is the whole field and costs 30-100 us up to 10k characters (Mac VM), so a window would only add bookkeeping. Shared with the iPhone: the observation idea (a read matches, is the queue's own write landing, is the user's change, or is unknown), `SpanPlanner`, `FieldMerge`, `HostLatency`, and the cleanup attach (`perSentence`, shadow ask).
- **Notifications say "look again".** `TextTarget.observeChanges` registers `AXObserver` value and selection notifications (main run loop, followed across rebinds, released on `reset`). They arrive 1-15 ms after user keys, synthetic keys and AX writes, one to several per operation (Edge coalesces a burst), so they are never counted: the queue reads after one and classifies the read. A field that sends none (`MacFieldHost.notifies` stays nil) is read every step as before. On a host that notifies, a step is planned from the shadow without a read while it is clean: no notification since the last read, no op, key, burst or late landing of ours in flight, no real input, not older than 1 s (`shadowMaxAge`, an estimate that guards a missed notification); otherwise it reads, which also checks our own chunk. A notification that arrives while the drain loop waits ends the wait early (production clock only; a rig that injects `sleep` waits plainly, so its virtual clock advances).
- **One chunk, one check.** A hunk's deletes and inserts are one chunk. Native: one AX replace (the delete run and the first piece) plus the entrance's further inserts at the caret, then one check by value within the field's learned deadline (`HostLatency`, kept per field in `MacFieldHost` next to `CaretJumps`). Keyed (Chromium, Electron): the chunk's keys go out back to back, each through the key guards without reading (`TextTarget.pressFollowing` skips only the selection guard), then one check of the text, tail, length and caret within the deadline. A check that runs out does not retry key by key: `settleBurst` reads how far the host got (keys apply in order), waits up to `keyLateLimit` for the rest, and when a key was dropped adopts the window as the region's text if the text around it is intact, then plans the remainder. Two failed burst checks in a row make the field fall back to verified keys (one key in flight, read back each; learned per field). Line breaks and tabs are never burst: they are pasted, one verified key.
- **Entrance (#300).** Every chunk's insert (speech, revisions, cleanup) enters as a very fast burst of `ChunkEntrance` pieces (one piece per 60 Hz frame, graphemes grouping beyond the cap), native as AX inserts at the caret, keyed as unicode keys spaced by the gap. The caret move to the chunk's place is not animated. Reduce Motion or a flush writes the insert whole. A user key waits only for the running burst; a user key between native pieces ends the burst and the rest is planned from the next read. The old per-grapheme typewriter pace and its repeated constants in `KeystrokePlanner` are gone (movement costs stay).
- **Latency bounds (derived from the Mac VM, 2026-11-17; labelled estimates).** Cold deadline 250 ms (above every measured burst: 34-102 ms after posting), floor 30 ms, ceiling 1 s, 2 ms per delete (Edge showed 19 backspaces in 37-53 ms). `MacFieldHost.latencyBounds`.
- **An interrupted keyed edit (round 3).** A real key cannot be held back in a keyed field, so the contract is about what follows it. When the user's key lands at the caret an edit of ours left mid-text (a keyed unit's jump; the field's length changed by what the caret moved by), the edit stops (its changed text is its own, the rest is dropped as the user's), the user keeps the caret they typed at, and the run's next words go where the caret was before our edit, mapped through the key (`runHome`), never at the caret our edit left behind. Cleanup units are separate queue steps: each key passes the guards, so a real key stops the rest of a cleanup at the next key and nothing of it goes out until `keyQuiet`. A burst met by real keys (or whose surroundings changed) is not retried or dropped whole: once the host's deadline has passed (interrupted) or the late limit (otherwise), the furthest of its states that the field shows once is the region's own text (`adoptFragment`), and the rest goes on from its end; the user's text is never part of it. Not covered: user keys inside our fragment, or a fragment too short to tell from the user's text.
- **Host rewrites (round 3).** With no real key or click since a native write, a field that shows the written text in another case, or with the one word the write completed replaced (Safari's autocapitalisation of "anna", autocorrect), is the host's own rewrite: the region takes the host's version as landed and keeps it in its desired text (`rewrites`), so the take goes on and the raw word is never typed over it. A word replaced elsewhere is still the user's change. When a write failed midway and the host took more or less than the queue counted, the same letters in other cases as the region's own intended text count too; a field that is simply unchanged never does.
- **Cleanup like the iPhone.** The Mac attaches `perSentence`: a pass starts from the queue's shadow (`MacFieldEditor.shadow`, no flush or read; answered once the queue typed every word it holds), bounded to the take (`latestRunSpan`), with a whole-take pass using the raw words at stop and after a 3 s pause. The shadow's text is recorded under the pass's id; when the result arrives `MacFieldEditor.apply` rebases it with `FieldMerge` through everything since (the field's text wins: the user's typing, speech typed meanwhile), so it lands only in untouched words; an unreadable field is `unplaceable` (one fresh pass), a result whose words all changed lands nothing. The merged edit goes through `queue.edit`.
- **Recovery.** `recoverTarget` journals the exact refusal (`changed`, `ambiguous`, `uncorroborated`, `cancelled`, `unrestored`, `unreadable`), no longer one lumped reason.

### Mac kept, with reasons

- `placeGrace` 400 ms: how long an edit that cannot be found waits before recovery; unmeasured, shared with Magic's placement.
- `waitTick` 50 ms: the pause between steps of a blocked field (focus lost, modifier held); no longer used after a failed check that already waited its deadline.
- `keyConfirm` 400 ms, `keyPoll` 4 ms, `keyLateLimit` 1 s, `caretWait` 150 ms: the verified-key fallback and the burst's late-key window; Chromium reports a caret late.
- `keyQuiet` 600 ms: after the user's real key no key goes out until their input rested; a synthetic key moves the field's own caret.
- `caretSetConfirm` 60 ms / `caretSetLateLimit` 300 ms, `moveStable` 40 ms, `arrowRun`, `jumpRounds`, `keySettle` 5 ms: caret movement proof, unchanged and unmeasured.
- A burst's later keys also probe that the field still reads and has focus (`sendFollowingKey`; reads cost 30-100 us): a field that stops reading mid-burst ends the burst at that key, the rest goes by recovery or the panel. Learned memory per field (`FieldMemory`) forgets the least recently written field first; an arbitrary eviction made seeded sessions order-dependent (seed 15).
- Not done: the notification-based learning beyond `notifies`, a `<ul>` list fake (markers counted in offsets, `AXStringForRange` failing, `AXSelectedText` ignored), and the Edge list-editor behaviour that made the 2026-11-17 journals drop: `Tests/LegatoMacTests/Fixtures/journal-edge-cm-*.jsonl` replay through the existing hidden-break reader and land, so that failure was not reproduced by a fake and stays unverified on Edge itself.

- **Focus by editor.** A take binds the element focused when it starts and types at its caret. Chromium recreates a web field's element when the page rewrites its DOM (Enter, a new list line, a re-render) and moves focus between nested editable elements, so the bound element can stop reading as focused while the user types on in the same editor. Then the queue binds the focused element if it is the same editor: the element itself, one inside it or its editable root, one under the same editable root, or a fresh element in its place (same window, role, DOM id and classes, x and width). While the take has typed nothing, any editable text field of the same app is bound, and the take starts at its caret. Typed work moves by the one shift under which all of it shows. Another app or another field after typing waits up to 3 s; then each take drops once, whole, with one message, and its later versions are ignored. The same editor in focus is never a drop: if it stops reading (below), the take goes on in the panel.
- **Reading web editors (`AXFieldReader`).** Chromium exposes an editor's text in two spaces. Its value and character count have a line break between every two lines, but its selection, `AXStringForRange` and text markers leave out the break between two adjacent non-empty lines: a tight Markdown list in CodeMirror, `<li>` or `<p>` blocks in a contenteditable. Reading the whole count then fails. Such an editor reads as its value, with the selection mapped into it. Where one selection offset stands for the end of a line and the start of the next, the text marker before the caret decides. A focused element that exposes nothing reads through its editable root (`AXEditableAncestor`, `AXHighestEditableAncestor`). An editor that reads neither way dictates through the panel from the start.
- **Unreadable mid-take.** When the bound editor has focus but stops reading for 0.6 s, its pending dictation goes on in the panel for the rest of the take. What was typed live stays; the panel shows the rest as it grows and is cleaned, and types it once at the end with synthetic keys. Nothing is dropped and no notice shows.
- **Keystroke fields.** Chromium web content (Chrome, Edge, their installed web apps, Electron) exposes its text once Legato reads the application's role, but ignores Accessibility text writes. The queue edits it live with synthetic keys, one in flight: Backspace to delete, one grapheme per Unicode key to type. Line breaks and tabs are pasted, never Return or Tab keys, and the clipboard comes back.
- **Value first.** An edit key counts when the field's value shows it: the expected text at its place, what followed still after it, and the length. Chromium reads an empty last line as a placeholder line break that goes when the line gets text, so one trailing line break more or less at the end of the field is the same text. Its caret can report late after a key on an empty line or at a block start: a key whose text shows is never sent again; the queue waits for the caret, and if the caret still reads as before the key, one arrow makes the field report afresh before anything else goes out. A key whose text does not show is not sent again until it shows or a second has passed.
- **Jumps.** The caret goes straight to a change and back; each jump is one verified step before the edit. Where the field honours an Accessibility caret set, the queue uses it, after one arrow proved that the real caret followed (Chromium may report a set it did not make). Otherwise it hops: Command+Up/Down to the field's start or end and Command+Left/Right to the caret's own line start or end when the target is there or a short arrow run from it, Option+Left/Right while the next word boundary is not past the target, then runs of up to eight plain arrows. A jump that lands off its prediction is corrected from where the field reports the caret, within four correction rounds, then arrows only; a hop that missed is not repeated toward the same target. What works (the caret set, and each hop's hits and misses) is remembered per field across takes.
- **Keystroke planning.** As on iOS, a word that changes by half or more is retyped whole, changes a few characters apart merge into one span, and each span takes minimal edits or a block delete and retype, whichever costs less. A caret move costs one key per grapheme up to the price of a jump, so far changes stay minimal. A small change types at a calm 30 ms per key and spreads a larger one over about 0.7 s; once 14 or more graphemes are waiting (typing is behind speech, about 15 characters a second) every key goes at the 5 ms floor, 3 ms in an Accessibility-writable field. A run of up to 4 typed graphemes (8 when behind) is verified by the caret alone, one read per key, and the run's text, tail and length by one read after its last key; each key still passes the guards below, and a move, a Backspace or a failed key ends the run. Moves and edits settle briefly between each other.
- **Key guards.** Before every key and caret set: the bound element is focused in the frontmost app, no real key or click since the key was planned, no modifier held (keys only), and the selection is still where it was read. Hops press the left-hand Option or Command around their arrow; Right Option and Right Command stay Legato's shortcuts. A failed guard sends nothing; the queue reads again and replans. A held modifier (push-to-talk) only pauses it. Every synthetic key-down has its key-up, and a sequence goes out whole or not at all.
- **Pill controls and history.** A take typing live into its field shows only a compact listening state in the notch pill, with Magic (the Magic hotkey), Undo and Redo. Undo takes Legato's last finished change (a take, with its cleanup, or a Magic edit) back through the queue as minimal edits; Redo puts the last undone change back. Both are disabled when there is nothing to do, and new dictation or a new edit ends the redo. The pill never becomes key or main, and its area is excluded from the real-input count, so a click on it neither moves focus nor trips the key guards.
- **Panel mode.** Unreadable fields show the take in the notch panel; Settings can choose the panel for every field ("Always dictate in the panel, insert when done", `dictation.panel` = `always`). The panel shows the take as it grows and is cleaned, and nothing touches the field until the take is final. A field the queue can edit (Accessibility or verified keys) then gets the text once through the queue, as one insert at the user's caret typed at the fast end of the pace. An unreadable field gets one typed insert with synthetic keys at the end, while the same app is in front and no real key was pressed.
- **Kept text (Mac).** The pill's "Text kept here" holds only words the field never got (`DictationPanel.recovery`), as on iPhone: a panel insert that could not be confirmed, the untyped rest of an interrupted one, a run dropped before its words stood, a refused Magic result. A take that landed keeps nothing; a cleanup the field refused only restyled words already there, so its drop keeps nothing either (`MacFieldEditor.dropped`). It is one item, the latest: a later session's kept text replaces it. Long text scrolls inside the pill, whose height is capped at half the screen, so Copy and Dismiss stay in reach.

Debug and internal builds journal every queue event with full text in `~/Library/Caches/<bundle id>/edit-journal.jsonl`.

A cleanup with several close changes is several chunks; merging them (`SpanPlanner.mergeGap`) is not applied on the AX path. The 5 ms typewriter gap and the verified-key pipelining are removed.

## Recovery

Before applying a Magic rewrite, Legato saves the original and generated result together in History. The same entry records whether application succeeded, was refused or could not be confirmed. A recovery-save failure prevents the edit from being sent.

History copies the generated result by default. Its context menu offers Copy original for a rewrite. Both versions remain available after a read or write failure, without another successful field walk. Undo is offered only when a known resulting scope can be verified. Explicit instruction retries use a fresh identity and revoke the previous attempt.

### What this means for the Mac shadow

- AX reads are cheap and flat in length up to 10,000 characters (30–100 µs, one outlier of 104 ms for `AXStringForRange` of everything in Safari contenteditable); a whole-field read per step is not a cost problem in the VM. Polling the value at one read per millisecond is safe; the cost is the number of reads, not their size.
- Value and selection notifications arrive in all three hosts, for user keys, synthetic keys and AX writes, within 1–15 ms. TextEdit and Safari send one or more per operation (several per write in Safari, with a stale selection ~100 ms late); Edge coalesces a burst into a few. A shadow cannot count notifications; it must read the value after the first notification of an operation and again after a short quiet period.
- A hunk write on the native path is visible to the next read at once (TextEdit, Safari textarea and contenteditable via `AXTextOperation`) except an `AXValue` replace in Safari, which shows up 7–25 ms later.
- Chromium ignores `AXSelectedText` and takes `AXValue` and the selection range: the keyed path is needed for hunks; a burst of keys lands in order and the value shows the end state 34–102 ms after posting (up to 120 strokes), so one check per chunk is enough. In a list editor the keys must not include a line-break key event.

- A real user key arriving while a hunk is written (the hold above is the app's own lease duration; no key was injected into it).
- Real CodeMirror, a real list editor (a web app's) and Chrome: only synthetic pages and Edge. No Electron app.
- Documents past 10,000 characters, long lines, and reads while the host is busy (typing, rendering); the figures are from an idle guest.
- Whether an `AXValue` write in Chromium fires the page's `input` event or keeps the undo stack (the text did change on screen).
- Notifications by their app-level registration were collected only for hunk writes (they match the element-level ones; registering both duplicates each).
- Hunk writes at 10,000 characters in Safari and Edge (TextEdit 1k and 10k, Safari 1k).
- How long a human key takes from keyboard to notification (VNC keys have an unmeasured transport delay).

## History: iPhone in-place sentence cleanup (#299 to #311, replaced by #312)

Until #312 the iPhone typed raw live words, then edited them in place with a per-sentence field pass. Device runs showed cleanup jumping the caret and removing text it should have kept, so it was replaced by staged delivery. The retired design, for reference:

On iPhone (`attachCleanupField(perSentence:)`, retired) a sentence is cleaned as soon as the segmenter closes it: the pass starts from the keyboard's shadow of the field and calls the model while the person keeps talking, cleans whole sentences only: from the start of the sentence the uncovered text begins in (the coverage overlap reaches back into the sentence before, so a correction across two sentences can land) to the end of the last finished sentence, with the field's text before the passage as context (`textBeforeCursor`, never edited). The words after it, a sentence still being said, are neither sent nor edited. **Every pass is bounded to the take** (device run 4): the passage is the take's own text, from where the take starts (`FieldShadow.takeStart`, its first non-space) to where its dictated text ends (`FieldShadow.takeEnd`, the end of its last piece; the caret when the shadow does not know). The user's text after the take (after the caret, or typed behind the take) is neither sent nor edited, and no after-context goes to the model. The user's part of the sentence the take begins in (a take dictated mid-sentence or mid-word, "slides and th|e budget sheet") is context only: in Notes the model was handed "slides and th … e budget sheet if you can." with the take, mended the word and moved the user's sentence behind the take. What a pass types at the take's edges is the take's (`ShadowDocument.Span.cleanup`, at a `dictated` piece's start or end): a sentence pass that made "Um. so, uh, the plan" "So the plan" typed its "S" where the take began, and the take's start stood after it, so every later pass sent "o the plan …", got "So the plan …" back and put another "S" in front (device run 6: "SSSo the plan"); a period added after the take's last word, or a word in front of its first, would likewise have been added again by each later pass. Without a known start the pass starts at the field's start, as before; a pass with no new finished sentence sends nothing (`noSentence`). Halves cleaned after a guard rejection get the text before them as context too. The result waits for the next gap between words (`canApply`, the queue's edit lane) to go in as targeted chunks. The pass at the stop, and one in a pause of 3 s after an endpoint (`DictationPipeline.wholeTakePause`, unmeasured: a guess between a breath and the speaker being done), covers the whole take: from the take's start to its end (above; the field's start when the shadow does not know it), with the text before as context, and gets the take's raw recognized words (`rawWords`, the run's finals before any cleanup). Sentence passes tidy correction cues away ("Tuesday. Uh, no sorry, Wednesday." can stand as "Tuesday. Wednesday."); the raw words show them, so this pass resolves corrections across sentences. The prompt (`dictation-take-cleanup.md`) treats them as reference only; the existing guards stay and one more refuses a result that takes more than a few words from them that the text does not hold (`DictationTakeGuard.keepsToText`). A pause's whole pass runs once per change of the take; a guard rejection splits as before, the halves without raw words. A line break in the field (an Enter between dictated words, a list) survives a result that joined the lines (`FieldCleanupChunks.keepingLineBreaks`). A pass the field did not take logs why (`why`: `noAck`, `unplaceable`, `stopped`, `refused`; a pass that changed nothing: `cleanAsTyped`, `touchedChangedWords`, `alreadyThere`). **No read round trips (#299 round 4).** A live run's pass asks for the field's text with the run's next snapshot (`LiveDictationSnapshot.shadowAsk`); the keyboard answers from a marked shadow revision. For pending speech with a known anchor it can project the appended words before host confirmation. Unresolved placement waits for a stable snapshot. The reply carries the shadow's span boundaries and the adapter keeps a separate host anchor for later synchronization. The marked revision protects manual text already present and rejects a result if the mark no longer identifies its scope. For a settled take the keyboard answers once the snapshot's words are typed (`FieldQueue.shadow`, `FieldShadow` on `link.dictation.shadow`), with no pause, no walk and no wait for its own work other than revisions, and marks the read under the pass's id (`markRead(mergingUser:)`). **No pass is built on revised words** (device run 5): an ask is a pause for its run (`LiveRun.paused`), whatever the route's activity says, so a revision the recognizer made of typed words ("move too big" heard again as "move two big pots") lands through the ordinary revision lane before the answer; the answer waits for it (`revisionDue`). Before, revisions waited for the route's pause, which the keyboard learns later than the ask: the pass got "too big pots", the model made it "the too-big pots", and the revision was then dropped because the cleanup had touched the words. The whole-take pass asks the same way. A piece revised once and grown since is revised again against its last revision plus the words typed after it (`baseline(of:in:)`); those words are the keyboard's own, and read as the user's they kept the second revision out. While the model works the observer keeps checking the field against the shadow (the reports it gets anyway; a walk only across a seam). The result goes without a re-read. The keyboard computes changes against the marked revision and transports the surviving changes at the tracked host anchor (`placeThroughRead`). The receipt carries the verified merged result for History and Undo. A field that still matches takes it at once; a difference (the user's typing, speech typed meanwhile) is merged around with `FieldMerge`, the field's text winning, so the cleanup lands only in untouched words; an edit that cannot be placed can request one fresh pass if no shadow edit was accepted. Once accepted, a cleanup's scoped target is retained across host writes. The queue verifies the cleanup's mutations before continuing and can finish a uniquely identified partial write from the same target. If the committed target cannot be synced safely, the take records the intended result separately from any observed partial result in History when enabled. Each pass logs its phases in ms since the request: `shadowSentMs`, `shadowMs`, `modelStartMs`, `modelEndMs`, `applySentMs`, `confirmedMs` (`readMs`, `rereadMs` on the read path). Kept: the read and the re-read for buffered takes (words still to be inserted at the read cursor need the read exactly) and for the Mac (its read is the field itself). The link serializes read/edit exchanges shared with Magic, while model calls hold no link lock. Mac reads carry explicit identity: a newer read cannot supply an older result's cursor anchor. History keeps original speech separately from the field read and confirmed result; vocabulary learning continues to consume speech, not surrounding field content. Cleanup labels reflect confirmation: cleaned, kept as typed, or unconfirmed. The same typed outcome travels on dictation status to replace an earlier transcript-region notice; it cannot clear another run's, another field's, or a newer notice.

The queue still contains that path (`FieldQueue.shadow(of:)`, `ShadowDocument.EditIntent`, `FieldMerge.restrictingCleanup`, scoped cleanup edits): the iPhone keyboard no longer asks for it, and its tests and journal replays remain until it is removed (open item in the staged plan).

## Limits and verification

A keyboard extension cannot obtain the complete text or absolute caret position in every host. Structural boundaries, missing selection information, stale browser proxy windows and text typed by another keyboard can make an edit unverifiable. Such cases resync or drop with a specific status instead of writing at a guessed position.

The package checks cover the iPhone model's contracts (observer classification, identity, chunk planning at the caret and mid-text, hop correction across units, hops within sight across cut windows, a failed check that re-reads, key order around a chunk, the "bread" device journal from `fresh-phone-uat` replayed against the measured hosts), the queue's earlier contracts (rebasing, unit integrity, drops, a Markdown list typed take by take) and seeded stress suites narrowed to no corruption: the user's text survives, nothing is mangled, no dictated word is lost or doubled. `FakeField.host(.whatsApp | .notes | .safari)` gives the measured behaviour: windows and their cuts at line breaks, units, a dropped overshoot, Safari's stale first callback after a move, callbacks for moves only, a new identity on empty. On macOS a seeded stress test drives the queue against a fake Accessibility field with lagging reads, user typing and clicks, revisions, cleanup and Magic, and against a fake keystroke field whose keys lag, land late, drop and meet focus blips; it can show an empty last line as a placeholder, report the caret late after a key at a line start, take, ignore, delay or only pretend to take caret sets, land word hops a few characters off and wrap lines. In half the sessions the fake recreates its element or moves focus into the caret line's nested element, sometimes before the first word. Two more modes play the keystroke sessions in a tight list read like Chromium's (hidden line breaks, a focused element that sometimes exposes nothing so reads go through its root), and with the whole editor unreadable for a while or for good, where a take must go on in the panel and the field's words plus the panel's rest must be the take's, each once. A test replays a real Edge journal where the first key on an empty last line was typed twice, another the Edge take whose element was recreated after Enter and "- " (it used to drop every version as focusLost), and one types a list take by take with Enter and "- " typed by hand between them. `LEGATO_STRESS_RUNS` sets a long local run and `LEGATO_STRESS_SEED` replays one seed. Native acceptance uses the real Safari fields and keyboard extension on iOS, and the running desktop app against browser fields on macOS. In-process text fixtures do not establish browser compatibility. Offline speech and instruction fixtures establish field behavior, not recognition or model quality.

# Direct field editing

Dictation and Magic deliver through the field queues below. The retired composition document, draft Insert workflow and whole-field refine path are no longer producers; iOS uses lightweight dictation status alongside the whole-text dictation and instruction links.

## Shared owners

`DictationRouteSession` owns the engine, speech-mode router and Magic cut for both hosts. Capture and presence remain platform adapters. `DictationPipeline` produces versioned whole-text snapshots; `LiveSegment.joined` assembles punctuation once at the producer boundary. Both field adapters consume `snapshot.text`. Field cleanup reads the document through the same interface as Magic; the keyboard no longer reports split points for app-side transcript cutting.

`SpeechDelivery` owns recognition revision versus new-speech classification. The iOS bounded word-alignment policy was selected after replaying both previous classifiers over two recorded November 11 journal sequences (30 snapshots): their delivered text agreed at every snapshot. Mac's `FieldSpeechScheduling.Target` decides when to release that shared result and supplies the existing conservative revised-tail policy. The keyboard permits revised-tail aliases; Mac releases them in an eligible natural pause or at stop. The recorded replay and the differing synthetic contract are documented in [shared speech evidence](plan/shared-speech-replay.md). `SpanPlanner` owns span formation and pace; `KeystrokePlanner` supplies Mac movement/settle costs and translates spans into keys. Costs never widen the minimal grapheme diff.

`FieldInsertion` owns capitalization and both insertion boundaries. A separating space before existing following text belongs to the dictated region, survives revisions and is removed with its undo. Existing whitespace and punctuation remain in the host.

Each field queue owns its undo ledger. iOS `EditHistory` includes scoped Magic edits alongside typing; both the Magic inverse and keyboard Undo/Redo operate on that history. Mac keeps its existing queue ledger and inverse recognition.

`DictationTakeCleaner` owns whole-take timeout execution and validation. Field cleanup validates the text read from the document. Transcript-only fallback consumers retain their sentence-context or buffered streaming/retry policies. `CorrectionCapture` owns pending sources, arming, own-write suppression and input-origin attribution. iOS discards unmatched sources because its proxy window is bounded; Mac retains them for a later readable snapshot and bounds polling to the existing 600-second expiry.

Platform differences remain deliberate: iOS edits a shadow document in chunks checked against the proxy (below); Mac uses Accessibility or guarded synthetic keys. Capture lifecycle and Magic field-settling callbacks remain adapter responsibilities. iPhone cleanup runs per sentence during the take and once more at the stop (below).

## Field cleanup and natural pauses

`FieldCleanupSession` owns automatic field reads, cleanup, conditional application and confirmation. Both live and buffered field delivery use it. Buffered takes carry their pending raw words alongside a current field read; `FieldInsertion` places them at the read cursor. Live speech keeps flowing through its existing queue. Cleanup never sends a rewritten transcript over the field: it applies a `ScopedEdit` against what it read. Password, selection, unavailable or unconfirmed reads do not initiate a blind edit.

`FieldCleanupChunks` owns 60-word passages with a 12-word overlap. Later passages read the previous result's overlap and replace it, so overlap is not appended twice. Its coverage frontier advances only from field-confirmed results and follows user changes through the shared `FieldMerge` boundary mapper. Later pauses send only the overlap and newly uncovered suffix; speech arriving during model work remains uncovered. These are measured workload candidates, not validated semantic optima; [recording measurements](plan/field-cleanup-measurements.md) give the evidence and limits. A stop deliberately bypasses coverage and requests a complete pass over the readable field, including long takes. A model result waits while speech resumes. Before application, a new read confirms the field; `FieldMerge` keeps user changes and accepts cleanup changes in untouched words for complete-document snapshots. Bounded windows must still match exactly, since they cannot establish common document extent.

On Mac, `DictationActivity` consumes capture PCM before network backlog. It permits corrections only when a semantic endpoint arrives after 200 ms of 20 ms frames at or below -55 dBFS, and revokes permission on the next louder frame. This candidate was calibrated on one speaker's recordings, not independently validated as a general voice detector. Silence or token gaps alone never release edits. `FieldSpeechScheduling.Target` and the field queue retain their existing recognition classifier, cursor confirmation and edit units; only the speaking gate now follows activity independently of take lifetime.

On iPhone (`attachCleanupField(perSentence:)`) a sentence is cleaned as soon as the segmenter closes it: the pass reads the field and calls the model while the person keeps talking, sends the uncovered text with the coverage overlap (about the sentence before) as context, and its result waits for the next gap between words (`canApply`, the queue's edit lane) to go in as targeted chunks. The stop keeps its complete final pass. The iPhone cleanup read uses the queue's shadow without an automatic cursor walk. The link serializes read/edit exchanges shared with Magic, while model calls hold no link lock. Mac reads carry explicit identity: a newer read cannot supply an older result's cursor anchor. History keeps original speech separately from the field read and confirmed result; vocabulary learning continues to consume speech, not surrounding field content. Cleanup labels reflect confirmation: cleaned, kept as typed, or unconfirmed. The same typed outcome travels on dictation status to replace an earlier transcript-region notice; it cannot clear another run's, another field's, or a newer notice.

Transcript cleanup remains only for integrations without an attached readable field and for the Mac panel's buffered fallback, where no field document exists. The production iPhone and readable Mac hosts attach the shared field owner. Existing transcript-path contracts continue to cover those fallback consumers.

## iOS: one queue owns the field

Every write the keyboard makes goes through one queue per field (`FieldQueue`): the user's keys, deletes, space-bar cursor pans, word-row and undo edits, live dictation and Soniox revisions, typed lines, the host's text, cleanup and Magic. Nothing else inserts, deletes or moves the cursor. The one exception is a cursor walk that reads the field (an instruction's read, or exploring across a seam); it runs only while nothing of the queue's is on its way and the caret is back at the user's cursor.

### The edit model (#299)

1. **Shadow document.** `ShadowDocument` holds the field as known: text, caret, the user's cursor, selection and the tracked regions. The queue simulates every op it sends on it; the observer (`FieldQueue.observe`) feeds it every host callback, idle or not, and classifies the report: it matches, extends (more text than known), stitches (known text, at one other place only, with at least 16 agreeing characters) or is a seam (text the shadow cannot place). A new document identity is not proof of a new field: Notes and Safari issue one when a field becomes empty, WhatsApp once with no visible cause. When the report under the new identity shows the shadow, the queue, the take and the history stay (`identity-kept` in the journal); a seam starts over as for a new field.
2. **Chunks, not characters.** Speech, a Soniox revision, a cleanup and a user key are each one chunk, executed whole: a take's words go in as one insert, a revision near the caret as one delete run and one insert.
3. **One place per chunk.** An edit's change is planned when it starts (`SpanPlanner`: the minimal grapheme diff, changes up to three unchanged graphemes apart or in one word form one group). Each chunk is the last group still to do: hops to its place, its deletes as one delete run (n single `deleteBackward` calls) and its insert, then the hop back to the user's caret, so speech and keys can go between chunks. A single change ending at most three characters before the caret is rewritten from the caret (no hop; the few characters between are typed again). A replacement that would empty a line bounded by a real line break keeps the line's last old grapheme until the new text stands before it (a rich editor fills an emptied line or drops its list item).
4. **Hops only within sight.** A hop never goes past what the last report showed on that side (300 characters in WhatsApp and Notes, cut at line breaks; 100 in Safari); a target further away takes several hops. Where a window stops at a line break, one character past it is allowed when the shadow knows a line break there. After each hop the next report is looked at: the caret at the target, the chunk goes on; the caret exactly where the same move lands counted in the other unit (UTF-16 code units in WhatsApp and Notes, graphemes in Safari), the shadow takes it, learns the field's unit and the next hop corrects by what is left. A report that fits where the hop came from, or any place the chunk had the caret at, may be late and proves nothing. Hops that bring the target no closer twice in a row fail the chunk.
5. **No fixed waits.** A chunk's ops go out back to back on the proxy's immediate view (no host needs a wait between a hop and the edit, measured). One check follows: a chunk that ends with its hop back is checked by the read that comes with that move's callback, once one callback per move of the chunk came (the keyboard's own inserts and deletes make none; the host applies a burst in order). A chunk that ends at the caret (a key, speech) is checked by the proxy's view. A report that does not show the chunk is not a seam until the deadline, `checkDeadlineMs` = 400 ms (Safari's last callback for a burst came at up to 260 ms, native apps under 60 ms; Safari's first callback after a move still shows the old place).
6. **A failed check re-reads.** At the deadline the report becomes the model (`resync`: the user's cursor moved, a jump went nowhere, or the report is reseeded). What the chunk was for is planned again against that read; text that cannot be found again drops, and for cleanup the app runs one fresh pass on the text as read (`FieldCleanupSession`, `unplaceable`). Nothing is restored blindly: a lost op is never replayed from the model.
7. **User input wins.** A key waits only for the chunk that is running; behind a chunk whose check is still open it goes at once and joins that check. A word the recognizer's head showed only in part is finished in the key's insert.

### Kept, with reasons

- **The opaque-selection hold** (`hasUnverifiedInput`, set by `observe` for a `selectionDidChange` that shows no selection, or a `textDidChange` whose windows stayed as they were). Safari's Select All shows no selection, so an automatic delete there would remove the whole draft. Only plain inserts at the caret go (a take's words, the user's keys) until a report changes.
- **Exploring across a seam** (`FieldRecovery` request, `FieldReader` walk; only with `canReadAround`, never while speech is prioritized). The observer cannot see past the window; an edit whose text lies behind a seam is looked for by a bounded walk, else it drops.
- **The read walk's settle** (`FieldReader.walkSettleMs`, 45 ms). A walk verifies every window by two identical reads of the host after its own move; it is no edit path.
- **The quiet after a tap** (`FieldQueue.quietMs`, 250 ms). After a tap the keyboard's own work resumes once the field held still: ops already on their way can still land at the tap.
- **Tap repairs** (`explainTap`, `tapRaceLanded`, `strayInsertion`). A tap inside a chunk's landing window moves what is still on its way there. When the report has one explanation (the ops before the tap where they were sent, the rest from the tap), the shadow takes it, the keyboard's own stray insert is taken out again with ownership evidence (dictation's stays and the take is reported), and what it deleted at the tap is typed back.
- **The stop's block swap** (`settleFinal`). A fallback when a verified chunk of the final version failed: the untouched take is swapped whole only when the report shows its end before the caret.
- **The near-caret revision while speaking** (`nearChars`, 60), for hosts without the route's activity admission (`prioritizeSpeech` off); production hosts release revisions in pauses.

Removed by #299: the settle constants between ops (move 45 ms, edit 110 ms, move-after-edit 90 ms, the 115 ms transit), per-grapheme pacing on iOS (`SpanPlanner`'s pace is the Mac's), `deleteConfirmationMs`, the zero-offset stale nudge, the lag and revert heuristics (late reports kept as history, the revert onto an older state and its parked unverified edits), the park wait for edits out of sight (3 s), the 150 ms stable report before a new region, and the keys-wait-for-take hold (#268): speech now goes in whole, so a key lands after the words already heard without a hold.

The draft-cleared observation (`observeDraftBoundary`, task 9): an empty, text-less field in the same instant as the keyboard's own op is looked at again after the deadline (`FieldQueue.observesClearedDraft`). The `fresh-phone-uat` and `recheck-phone-uat` journals hold no such clear (every draft-cleared reset there came seconds after the last op, after Send); the guard follows the observer's deadline rule.

Known limit: with a host that applies a burst late and shows stale reports after a tap (the stress fake's harshest mode), a tap inside the landing window of two chunks can leave one cleaned capital at the tap and its old letter gone at the place. The user's own text is never lost there, and the take stays in History.

### Dictation and instructions on the queue

- **Lanes.** User input goes first, behind at most the running chunk. Edits come next, then live dictation, with snapshots coalesced to the newest per run. While a take is spoken (`prioritizeSpeech`), edits wait for the gaps between words.
- **Dictation regions.** A live run types at its region's end. Each take is a run of its own in the app: the mic key (or Action Button) begins it, and so does the bridge's first `dictation-debug-line` or `-tail` after a `dictation-debug-stop`. A take that begins while the previous run still ends waits until that run is complete; stops end runs in take order. Every take starts its first region at the user's caret; text typed by hand between takes stays in front of it. A key or a cursor move closes the region; later raw words start a new region at the user's cursor. A take that typed nothing yet follows the user into another field; one that typed something stops there, drops once, and its later versions are ignored. A revision or cleanup belongs to the run's own text: its changes map onto the regions where they are, and text it adds at the end of the take goes at the end of the run's last region. Regions split by the user's typing or moves are separate pieces: where a snapshot changes the joint between two pieces, the joint belongs to neither. The keyboard reports where the pieces start (`LiveDictationAck.pieces`), and the app cleans each piece on its own. A finished take keeps its regions until its cleanup came (for the last four takes). In a region the user edited, each change touching a word the user changed is dropped; every other change still lands (`FieldMerge`), leaving the user's characters alone.
- **Kept text (iPhone).** A take the keyboard cannot place (a drop of a run, unplaceable or when the field or keyboard went away before its text stood, or a cleanup that never finished) is kept in the App Group (`KeptTextStore`, one item, the latest) instead of a plain notice. The word row shows its end with Insert and Copy (Copy needs Full Access) and a close; after the user's next key a "Kept" chip stands in front of the suggestions and reopens it. Insert types it at the caret as one user-lane chunk through `KeyboardController`; Insert, Copy and close clear it.
- **Instructions (Magic).** A read for an instruction waits (bounded) until the queue's own writes have landed, and marks the model (`markRead`). The edit is placed through every change since that read: a user change inside its scope cancels it; the keyboard's own changes there never do, and are merged into the result where Magic's change does not touch them. Cleanup may change hand-typed text only when that text was in the cleanup's input.

Internal builds journal every queue event with full text (`EditJournal`; see `scripts/device/README.md`). A pulled journal replays against the queue in a test.

## macOS

One queue per field (`MacFieldQueue`) makes every change: live words and their revisions, cleanup, typed lines, Magic and its undo, and the panel's final typed insert. Nothing else writes to the field.

- **One chunk in flight.** Each chunk is planned just before it goes out, against the field as last read: select the spot, replace, then read back the text and caret before the next chunk.
- **Minimal diff, one hunk per chunk.** Only the differing characters change. Each hunk is one chunk: its deletes and inserts go out as one write at one place, planned from a fresh read of the whole field (the Mac's shadow) and checked once after it. A failed check re-reads and plans again from what the field shows; no blind restore. Only new speech appended at the end of a region is typed one grapheme at a time at the typewriter pace (off with Reduce Motion or a flush); revisions, cleanup and Magic are never paced. A change away from the user's caret is a jump unit: the caret goes back to where the user expects it. A user key waits only for the chunk that is running; the next read takes it in. Keystroke fields keep their verified keys (one in flight, read back each); an edit that is not an appended tail goes at full key speed, without the typewriter gap or pipelining.
- **Rebase, never freeze.** The user's typing and clicks show on the next read. A key or click closes the open dictation region, and later words start a new region at the user's caret. Regions are found again at their place or uniquely elsewhere, and pending work is replanned there. Only work whose text the user changed, or that cannot be placed, is dropped; the take or result stays in History and goes to the clipboard. Words after a region the user changed start a new region at the caret.
- **Focus by editor.** A take binds the element focused when it starts and types at its caret. Chromium recreates a web field's element when the page rewrites its DOM (Enter, a new list line, a re-render) and moves focus between nested editable elements, so the bound element can stop reading as focused while the user types on in the same editor. Then the queue binds the focused element if it is the same editor: the element itself, one inside it or its editable root, one under the same editable root, or a fresh element in its place (same window, role, DOM id and classes, x and width). While the take has typed nothing, any editable text field of the same app is bound, and the take starts at its caret. Typed work moves by the one shift under which all of it shows. Another app or another field after typing waits up to 3 s; then each take drops once, whole, with one message, and its later versions are ignored. The same editor in focus is never a drop: if it stops reading (below), the take goes on in the panel.
- **Reading web editors (`AXFieldReader`).** Chromium exposes an editor's text in two spaces. Its value and character count have a line break between every two lines, but its selection, `AXStringForRange` and text markers leave out the break between two adjacent non-empty lines: a tight Markdown list in CodeMirror, `<li>` or `<p>` blocks in a contenteditable. Reading the whole count then fails. Such an editor reads as its value, with the selection mapped into it. Where one selection offset stands for the end of a line and the start of the next, the text marker before the caret decides. A focused element that exposes nothing reads through its editable root (`AXEditableAncestor`, `AXHighestEditableAncestor`). An editor that reads neither way dictates through the panel from the start.
- **Unreadable mid-take.** When the bound editor has focus but stops reading for 0.6 s, its pending dictation goes on in the panel for the rest of the take. What was typed live stays; the panel shows the rest as it grows and is cleaned, and types it once at the end with synthetic keys. Nothing is dropped and no notice shows.
- **Keystroke fields.** Chromium web content (Chrome, Edge, their installed web apps, Electron) exposes its text once Legato reads the application's role, but ignores Accessibility text writes. The queue edits it live with synthetic keys, one in flight: Backspace to delete, one grapheme per Unicode key to type. Line breaks and tabs are pasted, never Return or Tab keys, and the clipboard comes back.
- **Value first.** An edit key counts when the field's value shows it: the expected text at its place, what followed still after it, and the length. Chromium reads an empty last line as a placeholder line break that goes when the line gets text, so one trailing line break more or less at the end of the field is the same text. Its caret can report late after a key on an empty line or at a block start: a key whose text shows is never sent again; the queue waits for the caret, and if the caret still reads as before the key, one arrow makes the field report afresh before anything else goes out. A key whose text does not show is not sent again until it shows or a second has passed.
- **Jumps.** The caret goes straight to a change and back; each jump is one verified step before the edit. Where the field honours an Accessibility caret set, the queue uses it, after one arrow proved that the real caret followed (Chromium may report a set it did not make). Otherwise it hops: Command+Up/Down to the field's start or end and Command+Left/Right to the caret's own line start or end when the target is there or a short arrow run from it, Option+Left/Right while the next word boundary is not past the target, then runs of up to eight plain arrows. A jump that lands off its prediction is corrected from where the field reports the caret, within four correction rounds, then arrows only; a hop that missed is not repeated toward the same target. What works (the caret set, and each hop's hits and misses) is remembered per field across takes.
- **Keystroke planning.** As on iOS, a word that changes by half or more is retyped whole, changes a few characters apart merge into one span, and each span takes minimal edits or a block delete and retype, whichever costs less. A caret move costs one key per grapheme up to the price of a jump, so far changes stay minimal. A small change types at a calm 30 ms per key and spreads a larger one over about 0.7 s; once 14 or more graphemes are waiting (typing is behind speech, about 15 characters a second) every key goes at the 5 ms floor, 3 ms in an Accessibility-writable field. A run of up to 4 typed graphemes (8 when behind) is verified by the caret alone, one read per key, and the run's text, tail and length by one read after its last key; each key still passes the guards below, and a move, a Backspace or a failed key ends the run. Moves and edits settle briefly between each other.
- **Key guards.** Before every key and caret set: the bound element is focused in the frontmost app, no real key or click since the key was planned, no modifier held (keys only), and the selection is still where it was read. Hops press the left-hand Option or Command around their arrow; Right Option and Right Command stay Legato's shortcuts. A failed guard sends nothing; the queue reads again and replans. A held modifier (push-to-talk) only pauses it. Every synthetic key-down has its key-up, and a sequence goes out whole or not at all.
- **Pill controls and history.** A take typing live into its field shows only a compact listening state in the notch pill, with Magic (the Magic hotkey), Undo and Redo. Undo takes Legato's last finished change (a take, with its cleanup, or a Magic edit) back through the queue as minimal edits; Redo puts the last undone change back. Both are disabled when there is nothing to do, and new dictation or a new edit ends the redo. The pill never becomes key or main, and its area is excluded from the real-input count, so a click on it neither moves focus nor trips the key guards.
- **Panel mode.** Unreadable fields show the take in the notch panel; Settings can choose the panel for every field ("Always dictate in the panel, insert when done", `dictation.panel` = `always`). The panel shows the take as it grows and is cleaned, and nothing touches the field until the take is final. A field the queue can edit (Accessibility or verified keys) then gets the text once through the queue, as one insert at the user's caret typed at the fast end of the pace. An unreadable field gets one typed insert with synthetic keys at the end, while the same app is in front and no real key was pressed.

Debug and internal builds journal every queue event with full text in `~/Library/Caches/<bundle id>/edit-journal.jsonl`.

Mac kept after the chunk change, unmeasured: the 50 ms wait after a failed check (`waitTick`, gives a slow field time to show the write before the re-read), the 5 ms settle between key moves and edits in keystroke fields, and the 5 ms typewriter gap for appended speech. Not measured: how long a user's keys are held during one hunk write in TextEdit and Safari (needs the Mac VM with Accessibility re-granted per build, a GUI step); chunk size is one hunk until then. A cleanup with several close changes is several chunks; merging them (`SpanPlanner.mergeGap`) is not applied on the AX path.

## Recovery

Before applying a Magic rewrite, Legato saves the original and generated result together in History. The same entry records whether application succeeded, was refused or could not be confirmed. A recovery-save failure prevents the edit from being sent.

History copies the generated result by default. Its context menu offers Copy original for a rewrite. Both versions remain available after a read or write failure, without another successful field walk. Undo is offered only when a known resulting scope can be verified. Explicit instruction retries use a fresh identity and revoke the previous attempt.

## Measured host behaviour (iPhone, 2026-11-16)

Measured with the raw keyboard probe (`scripts/device/keyboard-probe.md`, write actions) on iPhone 17, iOS 26.6.1, in synthetic fields. Raw runs stay private in `~/Library/Caches/legato/probe-299/`.

| | WhatsApp composer | Notes | Safari textarea |
|---|---|---|---|
| Callback for the keyboard's own insert or delete | none | none | none |
| Callback for a move | one `textDidChange`, 12–28 ms | one, 12–23 ms | two: ~10 ms showing the **old** position, then 20–30 ms with the new one |
| Burst at full speed (move, delete run, insert, move back; no waits) | 27/27 landed | 15/15 | 15/15 |
| Burst result visible in a callback | 33–51 ms | 26–41 ms | 130–260 ms, one callback per stage, in order |
| Window around the caret | 300 before, 300 after, cut at line breaks | 300 / 300, cut at line breaks | 100 / 100 |
| Unit of `adjustTextPosition` | UTF-16 code units | UTF-16 code units | graphemes |
| Move past the end of the text | dropped entirely (caret stays) | caret at the end | not measured |
| Back-to-back `deleteBackward` | 35 and 500 all landed | all landed; 500 take ~300 ms to apply | all landed |
| Document identity | changed once with no visible cause | new identity when the note became empty | new identity when the field became empty; one identity shared with a contenteditable on the same page |

Consequences for the edit model: no host needs a wait between a hop and the edit; the check after a chunk is a read prompted by the return move, because the keyboard's own writes produce no callback; a hop stays strictly within the visible window and is corrected by looking, since the unit differs per host and an overshoot can be silently dropped; a new document identity is not proof of a new field. The 65–120 character windows of 2026-11-08 no longer hold in these hosts. A Safari contenteditable is not yet measured (its identity changed during a 30-character delete run).

## Limits and verification

A keyboard extension cannot obtain the complete text or absolute caret position in every host. Structural boundaries, missing selection information, stale browser proxy windows and text typed by another keyboard can make an edit unverifiable. Such cases resync or drop with a specific status instead of writing at a guessed position.

The package checks cover the iPhone model's contracts (observer classification, identity, chunk planning at the caret and mid-text, hop correction across units, hops within sight across cut windows, a failed check that re-reads, key order around a chunk, the "bread" device journal from `fresh-phone-uat` replayed against the measured hosts), the queue's earlier contracts (rebasing, unit integrity, drops, a Markdown list typed take by take) and seeded stress suites narrowed to no corruption: the user's text survives, nothing is mangled, no dictated word is lost or doubled. `FakeField.host(.whatsApp | .notes | .safari)` gives the measured behaviour: windows and their cuts at line breaks, units, a dropped overshoot, Safari's stale first callback after a move, callbacks for moves only, a new identity on empty. On macOS a seeded stress test drives the queue against a fake Accessibility field with lagging reads, user typing and clicks, revisions, cleanup and Magic, and against a fake keystroke field whose keys lag, land late, drop and meet focus blips; it can show an empty last line as a placeholder, report the caret late after a key at a line start, take, ignore, delay or only pretend to take caret sets, land word hops a few characters off and wrap lines. In half the sessions the fake recreates its element or moves focus into the caret line's nested element, sometimes before the first word. Two more modes play the keystroke sessions in a tight list read like Chromium's (hidden line breaks, a focused element that sometimes exposes nothing so reads go through its root), and with the whole editor unreadable for a while or for good, where a take must go on in the panel and the field's words plus the panel's rest must be the take's, each once. A test replays a real Edge journal where the first key on an empty last line was typed twice, another the Edge take whose element was recreated after Enter and "- " (it used to drop every version as focusLost), and one types a list take by take with Enter and "- " typed by hand between them. `LEGATO_STRESS_RUNS` sets a long local run and `LEGATO_STRESS_SEED` replays one seed. Native acceptance uses the real Safari fields and keyboard extension on iOS, and the running desktop app against browser fields on macOS. In-process text fixtures do not establish browser compatibility. Offline speech and instruction fixtures establish field behavior, not recognition or model quality.

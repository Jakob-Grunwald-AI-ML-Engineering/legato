# Background voice filtering

Reduce background voices is an experimental, opt-in transcript filter for composer and direct dictation. It quietly reduces other speakers' words while prioritizing the user's speech. It does not denoise the microphone waveform or remove background sound from recordings. Meetings retain every speaker. It is off by default and adds no model work to ordinary dictation when off.

On iPhone, open Settings → Background voices; on Mac, open Settings → Voice. **Set up your voice** is a guided recording of about a minute of reading, alone in a quiet room. The sheet shows the first sentence and records nothing until the user presses **Start**; then sentences appear one at a time, with progress. A sentence moves on when most of it has been heard (about 70% of its estimated speech time) and the user then stops speaking for about a second; with less speech heard it takes about three seconds of silence, so a pause at a comma does not skip ahead. Neither happens before half its estimated time; **Next sentence** moves on at once. The bar under the sentence shows speech heard against that 70% and stays empty without speech. It never moves on by time alone, nor before the user starts reading it: past 1.6 times its estimate the sheet shows a hint instead (read the sentence aloud, or pause or press Next sentence when done). Each step starts when it appears on screen, so a quiet pause starts only after the last sentence's speech has ended, and the next sentence's part starts only when it is shown. A recording that has not finished after four minutes (four times its plan, if longer) stops with a message saying so. Before setup, Reduce background voices stays off and touching it says "Set up your voice first." The first setup alone enables the feature and turns Reduce background voices on; it downloads the pinned voice model. Sentences are in German when the first preferred language is German, otherwise in English. Setting recordings rotate through sentences the setup does not use: every started recording moves the rotation on, so successive recordings (Alone again, Car, a custom setting) read different sentences until the pool comes round.

After the first setup, **Settings** lists Alone (the first setup), Office, Train, Car, Home with TV on and In bed, plus **Add your own…** with a custom name; custom settings can be deleted. A setting is only a name: the user records it whenever they are in that situation and speaks naturally. Each recording reads about 40 seconds of sentences with two "stay quiet" pauses of 10 seconds (countdown), about a minute in all. The Add button stays disabled until a name is typed. The microphone route (built-in, AirPods or another Bluetooth headset, wired, the Mac's dictation microphone) is detected and shown on the row. Any setting can be recorded again at any time. A setting recording whose voice scores below 0.2 against every saved profile asks before saving: "This sounds different from your other recordings. If someone else was reading, record again." with **Record again** and **Keep it**; it never rejects, and the first setup has no check. The threshold is an estimate set low because the user's own voice on another microphone has scored 0.22–0.29, so it catches only clearly different voices (17% of pairs of macOS `say` voices).

Behind the scenes there is one profile per setting and route, computed from the reading parts only, never the quiet pauses. Recording a setting again on the same route replaces its profile; on another route it adds one. The filter scores each window against every profile and uses the best match. The user sees one voice: **Delete voice** removes every profile, custom setting and kept recording, and turns the option off. A profile saved before this change keeps working as the Alone setting with an unknown route; nobody has to record again. Stop active dictation, held microphone sessions and meetings before recording. A recording reserves the microphone until it is finished or cancelled. The outcome (saved, too little speech, no microphone, microphone stopped, microphone busy, ran too long) is shown in the recording sheet, worded for the setup or the setting, with Try again after a failure (a retry reads as many sentences as the attempt before it); other outcomes (a name already in the list, Delete voice, Save to folder) appear next to the control that caused them and clear on the next action. The recording's clock is the recorded audio: steps, the countdown and the labelled intervals follow the samples, and audio is collected off the main thread, so a busy device neither drops audio nor stretches the schedule. A recording ends as interrupted only when the microphone stops sending audio for six seconds. On a Mac, Automatic skips virtual inputs (such as BlackHole) when a real microphone is present; when the only input is virtual, it records from the system's default input. After the first microphone permission prompt, the Mac brings the Settings window forward again.

Recording audio stays in memory and is discarded once the profile is computed. Only normalized voice embeddings are saved locally (`profiles.json`). The exception is **Keep recordings for testing** (off by default): while it is on, each setup and setting recording is saved on the device as 16 kHz mono WAV with a JSON file naming the setting, route, device, platform, date and the labelled reading (with its sentence) and quiet intervals. Each reading interval covers its sentence from when it was shown until the speech ended; a sentence skipped with Next sentence without reading is not labelled; each quiet interval is the whole 10-second pause, other voices in it included. Audio between two steps belongs to neither. Only recordings whose profile was saved are kept; a rejected, failed or cancelled recording leaves nothing behind. Turning the switch off keeps the recordings already kept and says so next to the switch; **Delete kept recordings** (with a confirmation) removes them and leaves the voice and its settings. Kept recordings can be exported one at a time or all together as a zip (with the profiles) through the share sheet (on Mac also **Save to folder…**), for example by AirDrop to a Mac for `scripts/dev/voice-replay.md`. They are never uploaded by Legato, and deleting the voice deletes them. Dictation audio still goes to Soniox; transcript filtering happens before provisional or finalized words reach cleanup, insertion or History. Settings changes apply to the next take. There are no live identification, rejection or filter-status messages in the app or keyboard. Ordinary dictation notices and errors remain available.

The current policy prioritizes speech recall: uncertain matches, short speech and words without usable timing pass immediately. Only repeated strong evidence of a different speaker suppresses words. A recent positive match briefly protects the user's speech; that bounded grace expires across pauses so it cannot admit background conversation indefinitely. The filter has no pending-final queue and does not hold earlier words for a later identity decision. Missing or failed voice matching bypasses the optional filter so dictation can continue. Other voices may still get through, particularly during overlap or brief speech; this is not reliable speaker exclusion.

The runtime uses the pinned CAM++ v2 CPU model. Audio context is bounded in memory and cleared when the stream ends. Model files are downloaded only during explicit setup and verified against committed SHA256 hashes.

## Evidence and limits

Physical iPhone testing confirmed that the user could enroll and save a voice profile. The initial strict integration then failed product acceptance: it rejected the user's own voice and later dumped text that had been deferred. The user also reported TV and other people's speech being picked up during thought pauses. These findings drove the current recall-first, immediate-text policy and removal of live filter messages. The user accepted the revised physical iPhone build: their voice comes through and background speech is reduced. This confirms the tested room experience, not universal speaker exclusion.

Earlier focused Soniox checks and the native iOS Release build covered the initial strict integration. Their passing results do not establish acceptance of the revised policy or override the physical failures. Current automated results must be recorded separately after the revised implementation is checked.

An earlier offline replay through the initial strict production matcher and SonioxClient, using real public human recordings and handwritten token labels, admitted 14/16 target-speaker labels and 1/16 other-speaker labels. Off and meeting modes admitted all labels without model calls. Profile save, reload, deletion and silent-enrollment rejection passed in that probe. Those numbers describe the initial strict policy only; they are not measurements of the revised filter, live Soniox recognition or two-person acoustic acceptance.

The guided setup, settings and best-of-profiles matching (#350) keep the filter's thresholds unchanged. Unit tests cover profile migration, the replace-or-add rule, best-match scoring, recording files and the replay's word counting; a probe with the real CAM++ model enrolled synthetic speech from long reading parts. Neither is device or user acceptance, and no word-level numbers on real recordings exist yet.

The revised filter passed user acceptance on the physical iPhone. The measured spike remains at `spikes/only-my-voice/README.md`; its no-go still applies to the stricter reliability goal. The accepted scope is quiet background reduction that prioritizes the user's speech; the stricter speaker-isolation goal remains outside this acceptance.
